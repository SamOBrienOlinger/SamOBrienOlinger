Case study 03 / Perspective-based learning

Stopped: Both Sides

One encounter. Two perspectives. An interactive way to explore public rights and Garda decisions in Ireland.

The need

Who is it for?

People exploring rights, responsibilities and fair decision-making in the Republic of Ireland.

Rights and professional decisions are easier to discuss when their context is visible. The challenge is to explain what a decision means while recognising the different powers and responsibilities of each role.

The approach

A way into the subject.

Present fictional encounters from public and Garda perspectives. Follow a read–choose–learn pattern, give explanations and source links, then let the learner switch sides and compare the reasoning.

The experience

What people can do.

  1. 01

    Make a contextual choice

    Branching situations use fictional characters and immediate explanations to connect a response with its significance.

  2. 02

    See the other perspective

    Switch roles within the same encounter while keeping both sets of answers available for comparison.

  3. 03

    Reflect and replay

    Recaps, alternative answers and saved progress let learners return to a decision. There is no sign-up or timer.

Learning & next steps

What this demonstrates.

The project connects social research with interactive development. It offers a concrete example of how a difficult topic can become a structured, source-linked learning experience.

Specialist legal, community and language review, alongside testing with learners, are priorities for further development. The prototype is not accredited Garda training.

General information, not legal advice. Not endorsed by any organisation or An Garda Síochána.
