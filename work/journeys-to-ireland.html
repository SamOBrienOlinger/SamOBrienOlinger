The brief

The need behind the work.

Migration and settlement involve overlapping questions about work, study, family, arrival and rights. A single checklist cannot explain every set of circumstances. The learning experience needs context as well as information.

My contribution

I developed the concept, fictional journeys and interactive frontend, connecting character-based scenarios with explanations, reflection and original guidance sources.

The thinking behind the build

Three decisions that shaped it.

01

Make the explanation part of the interaction

A learner chooses an answer, checks the decision and reads explanatory feedback before continuing through the five stages. Twelve fictional characters offer different circumstances to compare.

The experience supports exploration and reflection. Completion and scores do not establish a person’s immigration eligibility or prove a learning outcome.

02

Save progress without an account

Unfinished journeys are stored separately for each character in the browser. Returning from the information hub restores the active journey; completed decisions and reflections can be kept in the journal.

Removing account setup makes it easier to begin. Progress belongs to that browser and does not automatically synchronise between devices.

03

Keep search connected to the content

The site search uses an index generated from the actual characters, explanations, resources and fact checks. Results take the visitor back to the relevant content.

This avoids maintaining a separate search catalogue. Decision results still respect the order of the journey, and the interface explains that sequence.

From idea to delivery

What was delivered.

An interactive learning site with twelve character journeys, five stages per journey, saved progress, reflections, an information hub, fact checks and keyword search. Language and reading controls are part of the experience.

What comes next

Learner feedback, specialist content review and fluent-speaker review are the next priorities. Evaluation should ask what people understand and can explain after using the resource.
