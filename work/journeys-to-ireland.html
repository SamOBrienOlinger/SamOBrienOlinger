Case study 02 / Interactive learning

Journeys to Ireland

Twelve fictional lives make migration, settlement and the choices people face easier to explore.

The needThe approachThe experienceLearning & next steps

The need

Who is it for?

Learners, educators and community groups exploring migration and life in Ireland.

Migration information can be complex, and a single story cannot describe everyone’s experience. A learning resource needs to connect personal circumstances with reliable information while leaving room for reflection.

The approach

A way into the subject.

Use fictional characters and a five-stage learning journey: plan, prepare, travel, arrive and build a life. Let learners make a decision, read the explanation, compare another choice and follow the supporting sources.

The experience

What people can do.

  1. 01

    Explore a person’s circumstances

    Twelve fictional characters offer different reasons for moving and different support needs. Learners can revisit decisions and compare explanations.

  2. 02

    Connect stories with evidence

    An Information Hub, site search and Fact or Fiction activities connect the story with practical guidance and original sources.

  3. 03

    Keep a reflection

    A browser-local journey journal keeps completed learning and reflections on the learner’s device. Language and reading options offer different ways to engage.

Learning & next steps

What this demonstrates.

The project combines narrative, decisions and reference material. It demonstrates an approach to turning complex subject matter into an experience people can actively explore.

Further work should include learner feedback, specialist content and language review, and checking whether the resource supports the intended understanding. Completion scores do not measure real-world migration outcomes.

An independent educational resource using fictional characters. It does not assess immigration eligibility or replace professional advice.
